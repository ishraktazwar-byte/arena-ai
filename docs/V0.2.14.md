# V0.2.14 — fresh drop selection within short plans

## Why this increment

Previously, collect_items required an entity ID and UUID when the provider made
its plan. A newly mined or harvested drop did not exist yet, so the planner could
not reliably include its pickup in that same plan. Waiting for another default
five-minute planning cycle could lose it to despawn.

collect_nearby accepts only `{expectedItem}`. The strategic model chooses WHAT
item to collect; deterministic execution resolves a current eligible target and
handles HOW. This is a reusable collection skill, not a scripted farming loop.
Existing collect_items remains available for deliberately selecting a known entity.

## Selection and bounded execution

- Exposed only by the existing collection policy. Autonomous-world mode needs no
  new setting; restricted deployment still requires collection permission,
  separately from farming permission. Model arguments cannot enlarge scope.
- Validates the named item against the active registry. Requires the existing
  healthy collection body (health/food at least 12, NORMAL risk, known vitals),
  clean cursor/grid/window and at least one empty normal inventory slot.
- Checks at most 256 entities per scan. Desired-item/eligibility filtering precedes
  the sixteen-result cap, so nearer unrelated items do not hide the desired type.
  The ordinary scan_items tool retains its original unfiltered observation contract.
- Orders observed eligible matches by horizontal distance, then numeric entity ID.
  Eligibility is not a complete route proof; execution can still reject terrain.
- If no match is available, waits in at most twenty intervals of up to 50 ms.
  Discovery also checks a one-second elapsed-time limit before and after scanning,
  and refuses clock regression/late discovery. Event-loop stalls may delay return,
  but cannot admit a target after this deadline. No movement is issued to search.
- Binds one selected entity ID, UUID and requested item, then invokes the existing
  collector. It does not switch to another entity after failure, chase indefinitely,
  iterate all yield stacks or run another provider request.
- Retains the original body/dimension and four-block origin scope through discovery
  and collection. Rechecks body, inventory and permission after waits and before
  guarded body writes. Existing route/footprint checks and five-leg movement bounds
  remain in force; there is no walking on farmland or other partial-height terrain.
- The registry caps the entire action at 12 seconds, including discovery and the
  existing bounded snapshots, movement and pickup wait. Survival ownership outranks
  it; cancelled wait/sync replies cannot start later movement.

## Evidence and limitations

Success still requires BOTH the selected entity's server pickup report and a fresh
raw inventory snapshot showing gain. Merely selecting an item, reaching it, seeing
it disappear or receiving an animation is insufficient. Results preserve exact
selected identity, observed counts, partial-pickup indicators and inventory proof.
COLLECTION-RESULT now includes the tool name to distinguish explicit-ID collection
from nearby selection.

`originClaimed: false` and `exclusiveCausalityClaimed: false` are intentional: the
selected item might have existed before the previous step, belonged to another
player or gained additional items from incidental pickups. Minecraft observations
do not establish ownership or exclusive causality. Already-issued actions and
incidental pickups cannot be rolled back.

A real StrategyController/registry integration test supplies one three-step plan:
harvest carrots, collect_nearby carrot, plant carrots. It starts with an empty hand
and no carried planting items; the simulated server creates a drop after harvest,
reports pickup of four carrots, then confirms planting with one consumed carrot.
The resulting inventory has three carrots and the cell has an age-zero plant.
The drop is reachable from safe solid ground at the farm edge; this is not proof
of arbitrary farmland traversal or a general self-sustaining farm.

Multiple drops (e.g. wheat plus seeds), seed reservation, late spawn/metadata,
already-incidental pickup, constrained inventory, poor geometry or hunger can
still prevent a plan from completing. A failed selector stops the remaining plan
and enters the existing scoped retry cooldown. No partial-plan recovery or queued
command replay is added. Sustainable farm management, cooking, general building
and the larger civilization systems remain unfinished.
