# V0.2.15 — maintained farms, reserved seeds, recovery and farmland traversal

This release implements the four requested farming capabilities **for existing
prepared plots and the four supported crops**. It is not a claim that the entire
civilization platform or every form of Minecraft farming is finished.

## Choosing a farm rather than scripting a life story

`manage_farm {x,y,z,crop,targetStock,reserve}` persists a typed intention. Coordinates
identify the center of a 5×5 plot; y is crop-block height, not the soil's block y.
The center must be nearby, known farmland, in the current dimension and permitted
by farming policy. The two counts are integers 1–64. The crop is wheat, carrots,
potatoes or beetroots. `stop_farm {}` writes an abandonment tombstone.

The model decides WHETHER and WHERE to maintain a farm and how much stock to seek.
The worker chooses the locally safe way to fulfill that intention. No automatic
house→farm→king progression, per-action human approval or arbitrary generated code
is introduced. Autonomous-world mode uses its existing scopes. Restricted mode
requires farming, collection and navigation permission; the worker does not enlarge
any of them. Management also needs persistent memory and enabled strategic AI.

## 1. Seed reservation

The active intention protects its configured planting-item count: wheat seeds,
carrots, potatoes or beetroot seeds. Ordinary food selection excludes reserved
units across stacks, and rechecks the reserve after asynchronous equip, immediately
before consume. The planner sees seedReserves; food-stock needs exclude those units.
Planting outside the managed plot cannot spend the protected floor. Planting inside
the plot may use the reserve—its purpose is to restore planted cells.

Survival is more important than seeds: **food below 12 or health at/below 6** permits
consuming protected edible items. The food threshold avoids deadlocking a hungry
agent below the worker's minimum food requirement. Unknown/revoked scope, inactive
management or abandoning the intention releases the active reservation. Reserves
are local policy, not a server inventory lock: death, incidental effects and other
actors cannot be prevented or rolled back. Existing starter recipes do not consume
these four planting items; any future consumer must also honor reservation policy.

## 2. Recovery from partial work

There is no persisted phase or replayable action tail. Each worker tick obtains the
current scoped intention, terrain, maturity, inventory and drop identities again.
For example:

- A harvest that removed the crop despite a failed result leads to replanting the
  empty cell, not another dig of the old crop.
- A seedling that exists after a cancelled planting is left alone, not replanted.
- Seed and produce drops are considered separately and collected with the existing
  stable-identity plus server-pickup/inventory-gain contract.
- A reconnect/restart reloads the intention, not an old entity ID, route or command.

Failures use a bounded 64-entry retry ledger: 5, 10, 20, 40, then 60 seconds, with
10-minute retention. A new observed world state can imply a different safe action
instead of retrying the old one. Bare cells without seeds stop further harvesting;
the worker will not keep clearing a farm while unable to replant it. A fully planted
mature plot can bootstrap one harvest without carried seeds, then must recover them.

General multi-step planning also supplies a non-executable partial-plan summary to
the next budgeted provider request. It records completed-step count, stopped tool
and a fresh-observation requirement, not old arguments. This summary is in-memory;
ordinary outcome history and farm intentions persist. It is not a general-purpose
solver for every interrupted task, and does not bypass provider limits.

## 3. Farmland traversal

`navigate_farm` reuses bounded local BFS: at most 169 searched cells, six-block
radius, twelve short legs and the existing 15-second registry timeout. It accepts
full safe support or the pinned farmland/dirt-path shape `[0,0,0,1,0.9375,1]`. The four crop
blocks must have the pinned empty collision shape. Crop-free and crop-covered
farmland can be traversed in either direction across its 1/16-block edge.

The worker never enables jumping or sprinting. A tiny downward settling velocity
can be accepted at a known soil edge, but upward jumps, real falls, water, hazards,
unknown cells, slabs and head obstructions are rejected. Every step resamples the
body footprint/route and current body/dimension/permission/risk. Cancellation after
a delayed aim prevents later walking. Position estimates are not server arrival
proof, and external pushes or other actors mean no absolute no-trampling guarantee.

Harvesting and planting now accept farmland standing height. Their sampled sight
checks can pass through the four non-colliding crops, including the plant occupied
by the body, but not solid occluders. Mining's visibility policy is unchanged.
Collection uses the same farm-capable ground profile; ordinary navigate_local and
emergency escape retain their previous stricter full-ground profiles.

## 4. Sustainable farm maintenance

The runtime runs one worker action at a time between strategic planning cycles,
without extra cloud requests. Local survival/combat keeps priority. The worker:

1. Observes only known, permitted existing farmland within its chosen plot.
2. Recovers eligible seed/produce stacks, preflighting direct pickup terrain and
   choosing a freshly validated farm approach when a detour is needed.
3. Refills observed empty cells before harvesting more plants.
4. Approaches ripe crops before harvesting, reducing distant/unreachable drops.
5. Harvests when produce stock or seed reserves need replenishing.
6. Waits through immature growth and stops harvesting at the requested stock.
7. Resumes production when stock is consumed, with fresh checks each time.

For carrots/potatoes, the target is usable produce **plus** the planting reserve.
For wheat/beetroot, produce and seeds are accounted separately. The worker remains
local: being farther than eight blocks from the plot center, on another level or
in another dimension suspends work. Individual navigation/collection retain their
own smaller bounds. It does not walk indefinitely toward an unloaded farm.

Status and work-result diagnostics expose waiting, shortages, blocked harvest or
replanting, inaccessible yield, full inventory, unsafe conditions and fulfilled
stock. Runtime shutdown drains worker/memory operations. Disabling AI (no provider
object) does not silently activate a previously saved farm. As before, console
`stop` cancels the current action, not a durable intention; automatic work may be
reconsidered. `quit` stops the runtime; a chosen `stop_farm` abandons the intention.

This is sustainable **maintenance**, conditional on a functioning farm and reachable
yield—not creation of suitable terrain. There is no tilling, irrigation, light
installation, cooking, storage automation, chunk loading, ownership fencing or
multi-agent scheduling. Low light, dry/destroyed soil, lost seeds, full inventories,
other actors and server latency can stop production. The worker never invents
seeds, assumes growth or treats missing inventory evidence as verified pickup.

## Persistence, cancellation and validation

Memory schema 12 reads schemas 1–11. Up to eight world/dimension farm-intention
contexts are protected within the existing 500-record cap, separately from supply
objectives. Intentions contain only validated crop/plot/count fields and survive
history churn. Unsupported future schemas and malformed records fail closed.

An intention write admitted before cancellation can finish on disk afterward;
there is no claim that cancellation rolls back an issued disk write or server
interaction. No action follows it without current body, scope and world checks.
Crop/server and inventory confirmations retain their earlier non-exclusive-causality
limitations. Fresh local world reads can themselves be optimistic; primitive
success still requires its server evidence. Nothing claims atomic world ownership.

Automated tests cover pinned collision contracts, mixed soil edges, unknown and
malformed terrain, crop-covered traversal, interrupted aim/walking, planting and
harvesting while standing among crops, scoped intent schemas, reserve/eating races,
nutrition override, multi-stack accounting, partial outcomes, restart recovery,
backoff and non-overlap, stock limits, multiple yield types, memory migration/churn,
runtime scheduling and disabled-AI behavior. A real-tool worker integration runs
three harvest→pickup→replant cycles, reaches stock, idles, then resumes after
consumption. Growth and server replies are explicitly simulated in that test.

No live Minecraft or real provider validation is claimed.

Local verification: syntax checks and 453/453 tests passed. Three repeated Node 22
runs and one Node 24 run passed with test concurrency 8.
